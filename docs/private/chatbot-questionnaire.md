# Questions for Steven's chatbot knowledge

This is a private authoring worksheet. It is ignored by Git. The questions below are prompts for Steven, not claims about him or content to index. Start with a few questions you can answer well; completing the entire worksheet is optional.

Keep original documents and eventual approved notes in the separate knowledge folder described in [the knowledge guide](./chatbot-knowledge-guide.md). That folder and the sync tool still need to be created. Do not put private answers in a tracked code file.

For each answer, record the facts, a concrete example where available, the source or date, and whether the answer is approved for public chatbot use. Leave unknown facts and unmeasured outcomes explicitly unknown. Sharing a draft does not approve it for indexing.

## Professional background

1. How do you describe your professional role today in two or three sentences?
2. Which roles have you held, at which organizations, and during which dates? What did you personally own in each role?
3. What team sizes, disciplines, and management responsibilities can you substantiate? Distinguish direct reports from people you collaborated with.
4. What kinds of work are you most interested in doing next, and which preferences are suitable to share publicly?

## Technologies and technical judgment

5. Which languages, frameworks, databases, and cloud services have you used? For each, identify a project, approximate dates, and your actual involvement.
6. Which technologies did you use hands-on, which did you lead teams using, and which have you only explored? What examples demonstrate that distinction?
7. Describe a technical decision you owned. What constraints and alternatives did you consider, what did you choose, and what happened?
8. Describe a debugging, reliability, performance, or security problem you helped resolve. What was specifically your contribution?

## Projects

9. For each project you want the assistant to discuss, who was it for and what problem did it address?
10. What did you personally design, implement, or lead? What belonged to teammates or collaborators?
11. Which technologies were used, and why were they appropriate for that project?
12. What results can you verify? Include measurements only when you know their basis; qualitative outcomes are useful too.
13. What is the project's current status, and which public links or demonstrations may be shared?

## Leadership and collaboration

14. What does your leadership style look like in practice? Give one example of a decision or habit that demonstrates it.
15. Describe a time you helped someone develop a skill or take more ownership. What did you do, and what change did you observe?
16. Describe a disagreement or ambiguous situation you helped resolve. How did you listen, make a decision, and communicate it?
17. How have you worked with product, design, or other stakeholders to choose priorities? Give a specific example.
18. Describe how you balance delivery pressure with maintainability or team capacity, using an actual situation.

## Interview stories and growth

19. Which interview stories have you already written and want the assistant to use? Identify the original source for each.
20. For each story, what was the situation, your responsibility, your actions, and the actual outcome?
21. Which story shows a mistake, difficult lesson, or change in your approach that you are comfortable sharing publicly? State what happened without adding a successful ending that did not occur.
22. What did you learn, and is there a later example showing how you applied it? If there is no later example, say so.

## Personal interests and working together

23. What do you enjoy outside work, and which interests would you like visitors to know about?
24. What personal or creative projects have you pursued? What did you actually make or do?
25. What motivates you in your work or learning? Include an example if you have one.
26. What would you like a new teammate to know about how you communicate and collaborate?

## Public boundaries and maintenance

27. Which topics, names, client details, or parts of stories should remain private? Keep these exclusions in private review notes rather than approved retrieval content.
28. Which statements may become outdated, such as current employment, project status, or availability? When should they be reviewed?
29. Which contact methods and public links should the assistant offer when it lacks information or a visitor wants to speak with you?
30. What specific visitor questions would you most like the assistant to answer well? For each, identify the approved evidence that should support its answer.

## Suggested first answers

Start with questions 1, 5, 9–12 for one project, 14, and 23. They can support specific visitor questions about your role, technologies, project contribution, leadership, and interests. Missing answers remain gaps in evidence rather than invitations to infer facts.

## Reusable answer format

```markdown
# Topic

Review status: draft
Source or date:

## Facts I can verify

## Example and my contribution

## Outcome I can substantiate

## What is unknown or needs checking
```

Review each answer before turning it into an approved note. A future sync should read only the approved notes, never this worksheet or its draft answers.
